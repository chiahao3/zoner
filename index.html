What is drawn

Left column: diffraction space. Right column: real space. The orientation panel (ⓘ) sits on the right.

  • Diffraction: reflections at their excitation error sg (beam along −z). Kinematic, per-reflection two-beam (default), or many-beam Bloch wave computed when you stop moving. One convergence angle α sets the illumination: α = 0 is a parallel beam; otherwise disks of radius α are drawn as CBED once they are bigger than the spot size (zoom in to see small disks), with the rocking curve evaluated across every disk, so HOLZ lines and K-M fringes appear. Kikuchi bands are drawn for the strongest planes; the arrow points to the nearest zone-axis centre. Hover for the angle from the beam of the direction whose Kikuchi centre is under the pointer (deg, mrad and Å⁻¹; the same angle is the scattering angle of a spot there) and the α/β that bring it onto the beam. Detector draws the square detector, half-width = the collection angle β (corners at √2 β), about the optic axis; beyond it is dimmed with red stripes. Angle fields are typed in mrad and show the reciprocal distance θ·K in brackets.
  • Resolution stereogram: centred on the beam. Colour = projected 2D resolution of the image currently shown (depth sum or MEP slice) if you tilted there: the worst in-plane direction still resolved by zone reflections with |g| ≤ 1/(lateral resolution) inside the first lobe of the depth transfer. Scale from the lateral resolution to 1.5 Å; grey = worse than 1.5 Å (not atomic resolution). Hover for the tilt from the beam and the α/β needed; Rings draws dashed rings of total tilt. DP FOV matches the map's field of view to the diffraction pattern (the map edge is the tilt whose Kikuchi centre sits at the DP edge); zooming either panel keeps them matched, and choosing a map range ends it. With the holder range on, a red outline marks the reachable region; beyond it the map is dimmed with red stripes and zone markers get a red ring. Zones up to the index limit are labelled where the labels fit.
  • Crystal model: the specimen slab with slice boundaries and the current MEP slice highlighted (atoms outside it are dimmed), or a unit-cell cluster. The legend lists each species.
  • Real-space image: depth sum (default) or MEP slice, switched in the panel header; the stereogram follows the same choice. Phase σ∫V dz by Fourier synthesis of the perfect-crystal potential, each reflection weighted by t·sinc(t gz) and a Gaussian lateral PSF. The slice weight is the slice window blurred by the Gaussian depth PSF and cut to the specimen [0, t]: deep inside a thick crystal that is Δz·sinc(Δz gz) times the PSF transform, and a thin specimen tilts as well as its own thickness allows. Tilt smears columns by t·tanθ. Display: by default each mode uses a global range, the phase range of the same image on the crystal's low-index zone (e.g. Si [110]), so a mistilted crystal loses contrast as it would at the microscope; Auto contrast stretches each image instead. Depth resolution can follow λ/α² from the high tension and α (the λ/α² checkbox; typing a value unchecks it; α = 0 gives the 1000 nm cap).
  • Holder range (switch in the orientation panel, off by default): tilt freely until the pattern matches what you see, put in the stage α/β reading, then switch on (or press L) at any time. If the current tilt is outside the range and you type nothing, it is taken as stage 0, 0. The orientation is kept, α/β now read like the stage, and tilts are limited. Limits are symmetric: 35 means −35° … +35°.

Offline: download zoner.html, a single self-contained file that runs from disk with no network (for microscope PCs).

Qualitative tool: feature positions and symmetry are right; intensities are approximate (no absorption, perfect crystal).
